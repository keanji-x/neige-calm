Select exactly one terminal_id or task_id (exact attempt_id). Claim, release, or detach your terminal control connection. A human takeover revokes your previous control. Claim deliberately only when the user asked you to operate the terminal. For a scenario that starts and ends with an input, prefer calm.terminal.input claim=true on its first input and release=true on its last: one call fewer at each end, and the claim never displaces a human. Use this tool for a deliberate claim or release without a write. For claim/release, observe=true optionally includes a fresh text observation; wait_ms (0..20000; omitted means 0 for elapsed, 2000 for change, 15000 for signal and text), wait_for (elapsed|change|signal|text, baseline is the screen and signal seq when the action started, reported as wait.baseline_revision and wait.baseline_signal_seq), settle_ms (0..2000, change, signal and text), signal_events and repaint_ms (0..5000, signal only) and wait_text (text only, see calm.terminal.observe) require observe=true. Claim and release rarely change the screen: use wait_for=elapsed or a change budget of at most 500 ms here and keep long change budgets for program output after Enter. A release readback whose captured revision equals your previous observation on this connection omits the text array and reports text_omitted ("unchanged since previous observation <id>"), keeping every other field; claim readbacks always include text. A failed readback preserves the control receipt and reports observation unavailable. Claim and release receipts carry summary {action,readback,screen,settled,signal,repaint,matched,role,control_id,exited,claim,release} (nullable; the readback's wait and control facts): read it first as a digest of evidence, never as a verdict; the text block says the same in one line. Detach closes your client, leaving the Terminal card and program alive, and returns {detached,had_client,terminal_id,connection_id,terminal_session_id} without a summary. The full state is in structuredContent.
